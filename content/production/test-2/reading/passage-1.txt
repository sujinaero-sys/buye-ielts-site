The History of the Bicycle

The bicycle has undergone remarkable transformation since its earliest wooden prototypes appeared in the nineteenth century, evolving from a novelty for the wealthy into one of the most widely used vehicles on Earth. Aluminium frames are prized today for being significantly lighter than traditional steel frames, which improved both speed and portability. The earliest wooden 'draisine' bicycle was introduced around 1817. Pedals were first attached to the front wheel in 1861. The 'penny-farthing' design featured a dramatically larger front wheel. The invention of the pneumatic tyre in 1888 greatly improved comfort. Mass production techniques reduced the average price by roughly 50 percent within a decade. Modern racing bicycles now use frames commonly made from aluminium. The safety bicycle, with two similarly sized wheels, became popular mainly because it was far easier to balance and ride than the penny-farthing. Bicycle use declined sharply in many wealthy countries after the mass availability of affordable cars in the mid-twentieth century.
