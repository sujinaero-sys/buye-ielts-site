Microplastics in Freshwater Systems

While ocean plastic pollution has attracted global attention, scientists are increasingly concerned about microplastic contamination in rivers and lakes, which supply drinking water to billions of people. Microplastics are generally defined as particles smaller than 5 millimetres. A recent survey sampled 60 river sites across the region. The most common source identified was fibres from synthetic clothing. Filtration upgrades at treatment plants removed up to 90 percent of particles. Researchers used a 300 micron mesh to collect samples. The study was funded by a national research council. One recommendation was to install filters in domestic washing machines. The survey's main aim was to map the sources and concentration of microplastic contamination across freshwater systems. Even with upgrades, treatment plants remain only partially effective, since some particles are too small to be filtered out entirely. Reducing pollution at the source, the researchers argue, would require changes to clothing design and washing habits rather than relying on treatment plants alone.
