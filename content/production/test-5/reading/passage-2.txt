Vertical Farming and Urban Food Security

As urban populations grow, some cities are turning to vertical farming, the practice of growing crops in stacked layers within controlled indoor environments, as a way to shorten supply chains and reduce land use. Vertical farms typically use up to 95 percent less water than traditional field farming. Crops are commonly grown under LED lighting rather than natural sunlight. One facility studied produces crops across 12 stacked layers. Because conditions are controlled, vertical farms can operate for 12 months of the year. The main crop grown in many pilot facilities is leafy greens. High initial setup costs remain a barrier due to expensive climate control systems.
