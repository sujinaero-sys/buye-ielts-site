You will hear part of a lecture on the history of cartography and mapmaking. First you have time to look at questions 31 to 40. The earliest surviving clay map fragments were created in ancient Mesopotamia. Early Greek cartographers were among the first to calculate Earth's circumference. Ptolemy introduced a systematic grid based on latitude and longitude. During the Middle Ages, European maps often placed east at the top. The Mercator projection was originally created to assist with sea navigation. Portolan charts were noted for their highly accurate coastal outlines. The invention of the printing press led to a rapid drop in map prices. In the nineteenth century, thematic maps began illustrating data on disease. Aerial photography transformed cartography during the First World War. Modern satellite mapping enables real-time tracking of environmental change.
