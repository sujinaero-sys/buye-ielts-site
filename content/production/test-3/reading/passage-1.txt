The Story of Coffee Cultivation

Coffee is now one of the most widely traded agricultural products in the world, yet its cultivation began in a relatively small region and spread only gradually across continents over several centuries. Coffee cultivation is believed to have originated in the highlands of Ethiopia. Coffee was first cultivated as a crop on the Arabian Peninsula around the fifteenth century. Coffeehouses first appeared in cities such as Mecca before spreading to Europe. European traders introduced coffee cultivation to colonies in Java during the seventeenth century. Brazil became the world's largest coffee producer by the nineteenth century. Today, coffee is grown commercially in over 70 countries. Early coffeehouses functioned largely as places for social and intellectual gathering, where news and ideas were exchanged. Brazil's rise was closely linked to its favourable climate and the establishment of large-scale plantations across vast areas of suitable land.
