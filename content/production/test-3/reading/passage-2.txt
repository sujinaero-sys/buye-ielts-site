How Cities Manage Urban Heat

As global temperatures rise, city planners are increasingly focused on the urban heat island effect, a phenomenon in which built-up areas become significantly warmer than surrounding rural land. Dense city centres can be up to 7 degrees warmer than nearby countryside at night. Dark surfaces such as asphalt absorb and re-emit thermal energy. One common mitigation strategy involves painting rooftops a lighter colour. Urban tree cover has been shown to reduce local temperatures by up to 4 degrees. A pilot scheme in one city planted 10000 trees along major roads. Green roofs also help reduce a building's demand for cooling. Permeable paving allows rainwater to be absorbed rather than causing runoff.
