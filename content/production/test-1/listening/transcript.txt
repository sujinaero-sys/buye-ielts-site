Officer: Thank you for coming. The new recycling scheme begins on the first of March. Food scraps and other food waste go into the brown caddy, while garden waste will be collected once every two weeks.
Resident: Where should we take glass bottles?
Officer: Please use the drop-off point on Mill Lane. If you need help, the recycling hotline ends in 7124.
Resident: What happens if somebody puts the wrong material in a bin?
Officer: At first, residents who contaminate a bin will receive a written warning. The main reason for introducing the scheme is to meet a national recycling target set for next year; reducing landfill costs is a useful benefit, but it is not the main purpose.
Resident: My neighbour needs a replacement bin. Should she phone the hotline?
Officer: No. She should visit the council website and complete the replacement-bin form there.
Resident: And what about a broken sofa?
Officer: Large furniture items are collected by appointment only. They must not simply be left outside. The trial will run for six months, and then the council will review the results.

[Part 4]
You will hear part of a lecture on the history of the tea trade. First you have some time to look at questions 31 to 40.

Lecturer: Today we are tracing the development of the international tea trade. In its earliest international phase, tea travelled along maritime routes. By the seventeenth century it had become popular in Europe, where buyers valued it first for its medicinal properties rather than simply as a beverage.

As demand increased, large-scale plantations were established in India. Later, the introduction of the steamship reduced the journey time to under sixty days. This made supply more predictable and encouraged merchants to develop clearer standards. A new grading system for leaf quality was introduced in 1870.

During the twentieth century, the main auction centre changed: auctions moved from London to Mombasa. Today China is the largest producer by volume. If we measure consumption per person, however, Turkey is the leading country. The modern market is also changing, with demand for specialty tea continuing to rise.
