The Rise of Urban Beekeeping

Over the past two decades, beekeeping has moved from rural farms into the heart of many cities. Rooftops, balconies and community gardens now host thousands of hives in urban centres worldwide. The first recorded urban apiary in the modern movement was established on a rooftop in 1983. A single healthy hive can contain up to 60000 bees during summer. Urban beekeepers commonly harvest honey two times per year. One frequently cited advantage of city hives is access to a wider variety of flowering plants. Local regulations in many cities now require hives to be set back at least 3 metres from public paths. Community groups often host beginner courses lasting four weekends. Some ecologists caution that packing too many hives into a small area can crowd out wild, native pollinator species that compete for the same flowers. In response, several city councils have introduced registration schemes so that hive locations can be tracked and disputes resolved.
